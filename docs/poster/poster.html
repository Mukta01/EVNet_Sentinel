1Problem

Every public EV charger (EVSE) is a networked computer. It reports to a charging management system (CSMS) over OCPP, usually across the site's shared wifi, and talks to the car over ISO 15118. Flooding a charger cuts its link to the CSMS: sessions cannot be authorised or billed.

Makhmudov et al. [1] reported 98.4% accuracy with an adaptive random forest and drift detection. We set out to reproduce it, and to go beyond detection.

2Dataset

CICEVSE2024 (Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity): traffic from two real charging stations, idle and charging, recorded from a mirrored switch port.

  • 1,198,151 flows after de-duplication, 15 classes, 60 flow features
  • Attacks: 5 volumetric floods, 6 nmap scan types, ICMP and Slowloris
  • Senders: a laptop and a Raspberry Pi on the wifi, and a compromised car scanning over the charging cable

3The leak

Published model, accuracy98.4%
One timestamp column alone99.8%
Leak removed, macro-F10.558

Six absolute capture timestamps identify the recording, not the traffic: a single column (bidirectional_first_seen_ms) out-scores the published model. The drift alarms also land on capture-file seams, not real concept drift.

4Method

1

Remove leakage — drop the timestamps and src_port; de-duplicate flows.

2

Split — stratified train / validation / test, repeated for seeds 42, 1337, 2024.

3

Detect — six static models (XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest, Decision Tree, Logistic Regression, SVM) and an online ARF + ADWIN.

4

Test per attack — every class, every model, 30 × 50 unseen flows per model set; graded detected / right category / named.

5

Respond — a containment policy, replayed over every recorded attack (section 8).

5Why macro-F1, not accuracy

After de-duplication, floods are over 80% of the flows (99% on the published feature set). A model that names every flood and misses every scan still scores high accuracy.

Same model, published feature set

Accuracy — dominated by floods99.6%
Macro-F1 — every class counts equally0.70

Macro-F1 averages the F1 of each of the 15 classes, so a missed scan type weighs as much as a flood.

8Closing the loop

Detection alone does not stop an attacker. Sentinel recommends an action matched to how reliable the detector is; a person approves every action, and the chargers and CSMS can never be cut off.

10Conclusions

  • The published 98.4% comes from capture timestamps; corrected macro-F1 is ≈ 0.55 for every tree model.
  • Floods are solved (≥ 99.8% named); scans are data-limited, not model-limited — stronger models do not lift the ceiling.
  • A detector earns its keep in the response: category-level actions contain every recorded attack.

Next: session-level features to separate the scan types, and validation on a live OCPP testbed.
